# Agilex 27 widens tensor DSPs, skips FP8

Altera's 28 October 2026 Agilex 2X launch published real architectural detail for one family only, Agilex 27, and that detail answers most of the tensor questions directly: the second-generation AI Tensor DSP block has **two tensor columns per block, each computing a 16-element dot product, for 32 INT8 or 32 "Block FP16" multipliers per block with INT32 or FP32 accumulation**, up from Agilex 5's two 10-element columns (20 multipliers). The published data-type list is **INT8, INT9, INT16, INT32, BFLOAT16, BlockFP16, FP16, FP32, TF32 (also labelled FP19), CINT16 and CINT18**; **no Altera document mentions FP8, FP4, INT4, MXFP, OCP microscaling or "shared exponent"**, and the one block-floating-point hint, "BlockFP16", is never defined. The preliminary product table lists **14 Agilex 27 devices from 0.866 M to 17.6 M logic elements, 1,096 to 21,048 tensor blocks and 63 to 1,211 INT8 TOPS**, which divides out to a near-constant implied tensor clock of about 900 MHz. Memory gains a new 80 Kb M80K block that supplies more than half the block RAM bits on every device, and the HyperFlow network-on-chip hardens the Agilex 7 M-Series memory NoC into a full-chip system with 512-bit ports and descriptor-driven DMA. Nothing numeric exists for Agilex 25, no process node or foundry is named for any Agilex 2X part, and the only dates are Quartus Prime early access from mid-November 2026 and engineering samples in the second half of 2027. For inference, the block is roughly 1.6x Agilex 5 per clock and the smallest Agilex 27 already out-multiplies the largest Agilex 7, but with no sub-8-bit mode the family cannot match the 2x INT8-rate MX6 path that AMD advertises for Versal AI Edge Gen 2, and MXFP4 would have to ride the INT8 mantissa datapath exactly as it does on Agilex 5. One of the four research notes concluded that no lane count, format list or TOPS figure was public; that note was blocked from docs.altera.com and worked from press coverage, and this report follows the primary Altera documents instead.

## Primary Altera documents resolve the lane-count dispute

The disagreement between the notes is about access, not interpretation. The architecture note read the Agilex 27 FPGAs and SoCs Device Overview (version 2026.10.28, "Initial release"), the 16-page white paper WP-01358-1.0 by Chief Architect Ilya Ganusov and Graham Baker, the product table Gen-1088-1.0, and the two product briefs, all live on docs.altera.com ([Agilex 27 Device Overview](https://docs.altera.com/r/docs/d791593/current/agilex-27-fpgas-and-socs-device-overview); [WP-01358-1.0](https://docs.altera.com/api/khub/documents/hGPnldzZxr4wmKkmVpzs1w/content); [Product Table Gen-1088-1.0](https://docs.altera.com/api/khub/documents/XCwjkADTS8Ob_yREw1E4YA/content)). The AI-formats note and the availability note hit HTTP 403 and 404 responses from altera.com, docs.altera.com and BusinessWire, so they worked from ServeTheHome, Converge Digest, TechTimes, SiliconANGLE and Embedded.com, none of which reported DSP formats, dot-product width, device counts or TOPS ([ServeTheHome](https://www.servethehome.com/altera-agilex-27-800gbe-1-6tbe-hyperflow/); [Converge Digest](https://convergedigest.com/altera-agilex-2x-fpga-16t-ethernet-256g-serdes/)). Both of those notes said plainly that their conclusions rested on press text, which is the right caveat; the press simply did not carry the numbers. Where this report quotes a lane count, a device table or a performance figure, it comes from the Altera technical documents, and every one of those documents carries Altera's own "Preliminarily and Subject to Change" footer because silicon is a year away.

The same rule settles two smaller conflicts. SiliconANGLE wrote that Agilex 27 "software support is available now in Quartus Prime 26.3", but Altera's release says support "will be available to early access customers starting in mid-November", so 26.3 as shipped carries QuartusIQ, not general Agilex 27 device support ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/10/28/altera-unveils-agilex-2x-betting-on-ai-developers-to-expand-programmable-computing/); [Altera newsroom](https://www.altera.com/newsroom/agilex-2x)). And the availability note's statement that "no logic-element counts or device table for Agilex 27 or 25 were published" is correct for Agilex 25 and wrong for Agilex 27, whose four-page product table was posted on 28 October.

Throughout, four labels are used. Vendor specification means the Device Overview, white paper and product table. Vendor marketing means the press release, product pages, product briefs and solution briefs. Press means third-party reporting. Researcher arithmetic means calculations made from published numbers in the notes and in this report, which Altera has not stated.

## The block does 16-element dot products, 32 INT8 multipliers, and nothing below 8 bits

Vendor specification. The Device Overview's Table 8, "AI Tensor DSP Block Configurations", describes the INT8 tensor row as "Two tensor columns are integrated in one DSP block, with INT32 or FP32 output/accumulation" with the target application "Tensor dot products of 16-element vectors computation and matrix multiplication" ([Device Overview 2.3](https://docs.altera.com/r/docs/d791593/current/agilex-27-fpgas-and-socs-device-overview)). The white paper states the multiplier count outright: "New tensor modes provide 32 INT8 or 32 Block FP16 multipliers per DSP block, and new INT18 and INT32 modes remove the soft logic that complex integer arithmetic previously required. Overall, compute density per DSP block rises 2 to 4 times" ([WP-01358-1.0, p.10](https://docs.altera.com/api/khub/documents/hGPnldzZxr4wmKkmVpzs1w/content)). Two columns of 16 is 32, so the two documents agree. The Device Overview's feature summary gives the full type list: "INT8, INT9, INT16, and INT32. BFLOAT16 and BlockFP16. FP16, FP32, and TF32. CINT16 and CINT18" ([Device Overview 1.4](https://docs.altera.com/r/docs/d791593/current/agilex-27-fpgas-and-socs-device-overview)). The white paper's Table 4 compares multipliers per block against Agilex 7, and is transcribed here because it is the only per-mode density table Altera has published.

| Workload | Precision | Agilex 7 per block | Agilex 27 per block | Change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AI | INT8 (scalar) | 4 | 8 | 2x |
| AI | INT8 (tensor) | n/a | 32 | New |
| AI | Block FP16 (tensor) | n/a | 32 | New |
| AI | Bfloat16 | 2 | 4 | 2x |
| AI | FP19 (TF32) | 2 | 3 | 1.5x |
| Wireless | CINT16 | 0.5 + soft logic | 2 | 4x |
| Wireless | INT18 | 2 | 4 | 2x |
| Wireless | CINT18 | 0.5 + soft logic | 1 | 2x |
| General | INT32 | n/a | 1 | New |
| General | FP16 | 2 | 4 | 2x |
| General | FP32 | 1 | 2 | 2x |

Source: WP-01358-1.0 Table 4 ([white paper](https://docs.altera.com/api/khub/documents/hGPnldzZxr4wmKkmVpzs1w/content)). The remaining Table 8 rows in the Device Overview cover the classic modes: 9x9 INT at one-sixth of a block, 18x19 complex INT in one block, 27x27 at half a block, 36x36 in one block with a 72-bit result, 54x54 across four blocks, and a new native FP16 complex mode ([Device Overview 2.3](https://docs.altera.com/r/docs/d791593/current/agilex-27-fpgas-and-socs-device-overview)). The product table counts each tensor block as exactly four 18x19 multipliers on every device, which is the legacy Agilex accounting, so the "18x19 Multipliers" row understates tensor capability by 8x ([Product Table](https://docs.altera.com/api/khub/documents/XCwjkADTS8Ob_yREw1E4YA/content)).

Vendor marketing adds nothing numeric. The press release's single sentence is "Enhanced AI tensor DSP blocks accelerate compute in signal processing applications", and the Giga-MIMO solution brief says each block performs two complex INT16 multiplications, a 4x gain over Agilex 7 ([Altera newsroom](https://www.altera.com/newsroom/agilex-2x); [SB-1215-1.0](https://docs.altera.com/api/khub/documents/T4~~UVWU3Jd9rgKAZjWVZA/content)). Press coverage, as noted, carried no DSP detail at all.

Now the gaps, stated as plainly as the question asks. **FP8 in either E4M3 or E5M2, FP6, FP4, INT4, MXFP, OCP MX and the phrase "shared exponent" appear nowhere** in the Device Overview, white paper, product table, briefs, press release or product pages ([architecture note gap list, from the Device Overview](https://docs.altera.com/r/docs/d791593/current/agilex-27-fpgas-and-socs-device-overview)). INT4 is a notable absence because Altera's own Stratix 10 NX tensor block offered INT4 at twice the INT8 rate alongside "shared exponent to support Block FP16 and block FP12 numerics" ([Stratix 10 NX material](https://docs.altera.com/v/u/vuRNmNci66pfc_1eQlNd~A)). Accumulator width beyond "INT32 or FP32", cascade behaviour in tensor mode, the exponent-sharing granularity and width of Block FP16, and the DSP operating frequency are all unpublished, and no Agilex 27 DSP user guide exists yet on docs.altera.com. One internal inconsistency also stands: the white paper's 8 scalar INT8 multipliers per block does not match the Device Overview's "9x9 INT: one-sixth of a DSP block" unless those are different packings.

Researcher inference on Block FP16. The name is inherited from Stratix 10 NX, where it meant INT8 mantissas under a shared exponent, and the Agilex 5 tensor mode "uses an 8-bit shared exponent per 10-element vector operand and accumulates results in fp32" ([Agilex 5 DSP user guide, tensor mode](https://intel.com/content/www/us/en/docs/programmable/813968/24-1/tensor-mode.html); [arXiv 2607.13898](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.13898)). Because Agilex 27's Block FP16 tensor mode has exactly the same 32 multipliers per block as INT8 tensor mode, the most economical reading is that it is the same construction: a shared exponent per vector operand with the mantissa products running on the INT8 hardware. Altera has not said this for Agilex 27, and whether the exponent is shared per 16-element column or per block is unknown.

The TOPS column is internally consistent with full 32-multiplier tensor mode at one clock: 1,211e12 / (21,048 blocks x 32 multipliers x 2 ops) is **899 MHz** for A27F180, and 63e12 / (1,096 x 64) is 898 MHz for A27F008. ## M80K carries most of the on-chip bits, and in-package memory is never called HBM

Vendor specification. Agilex 27 keeps the Agilex 7 memory structure and adds a block: "Agilex 27 FPGAs and SoCs keep the proven Agilex 7 embedded memory architecture and add a new and larger M80K block for applications that need more on-chip memory" ([Device Overview 2.5](https://docs.altera.com/r/docs/d791593/current/agilex-27-fpgas-and-socs-device-overview)). Table 9 gives the three levels. MLAB grows to **768 bits** (from 640 in Agilex 7) in 32x24 or 16x48 configurations with no ECC. M20K stays at **20,480 bits** in 2,048x10, 1,024x20 and 512x40 principal modes with 32+8 ECC. **M80K is 81,920 bits in 8,192x10, 4,096x20, 2,048x40 and 1,024x80 configurations, with hard ECC in both 32+8 and 64+16 forms**, and all three block types support single-port, simple dual-port, true dual-port, simple quad-port, FIFO, ROM and shift-register modes ([Device Overview 2.5](https://docs.altera.com/r/docs/d791593/current/agilex-27-fpgas-and-socs-device-overview); [Agilex 7 Device Overview](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/docs/programmable/683458/current/internal-embedded-memory.html)). The white paper explains the purpose: M80K replaces the old practice of ganging four M20Ks with a shared address behind an output mux, provides "4x the depth at the same width, or 4x the width at the same depth", and is aimed at "bulk buffering, deep FIFOs, packet queues, lookup tables, and metadata stores", with Quartus free to map to either block because the modes overlap ([WP-01358-1.0, p.6](https://docs.altera.com/api/khub/documents/hGPnldzZxr4wmKkmVpzs1w/content)). Altera claims 15% to 40% more on-chip memory per device at equal logic count versus the prior generation. On A27F180 the split is 822 Mb of M20K, 1,096 Mb of M80K and 158 Mb of MLAB, so M80K is more than half of the 1,918 Mb total on every device, a ratio Altera's table shows but does not comment on. No eSRAM is mentioned anywhere; Agilex 7 had 18 Mb eSRAM on some devices. Port widths in true dual-port mode, M80K clock, latency, and whether M80K is four M20K arrays sharing a port or a distinct macro are unpublished, and there is no Embedded Memory User Guide yet.

External memory is specified more fully. The small devices (866K to 3.1M LE) support DDR5 to 9,200 MT/s, LPDDR4 to 4,266 and LPDDR5X to 9,600; devices from 4.5M LE up swap LPDDR4 for **LPDDR6 to 14,400 MT/s**. Controllers come three to an HSIO bank with 64-entry queues, 256-bit AES-XTS inline encryption and a pair of 256-bit AXI ports onto the NoC ([Device Overview 4., Table 12 and 13](https://docs.altera.com/r/docs/d791593/current/agilex-27-fpgas-and-socs-device-overview)). The white paper's Table 3 puts **38.4 GB/s per memory interface and 269 GB/s per device with LPDDR5**, "higher with LPDDR6", against 25.6 and 205 GB/s for Agilex 7 M-Series ([WP-01358-1.0, p.9](https://docs.altera.com/api/khub/documents/hGPnldzZxr4wmKkmVpzs1w/content)).

Vendor marketing, on in-package memory. An Agilex 27 "M-Series" is announced with "Memory-in-package, for greater data in less board area" and, per the NIC solution brief, "up to 921 GB/s of in-package bandwidth" ([PB-1026-1.0](https://docs.altera.com/api/khub/documents/D1hI5l2t7xc3Op2N9C1sYw/content); [SB-1213-1.0](https://docs.altera.com/api/khub/documents/MJjESyrW__pqrR1DLbgjCQ/content)). **The word HBM does not appear in any Agilex 27 document for Altera's own part**; Converge Digest lists HBM as "Not mentioned" ([Converge Digest](https://convergedigest.com/altera-agilex-2x-fpga-16t-ethernet-256g-serdes/)). The 410 GB/s per stack and 820 GB/s aggregate figures that surface in search belong to Agilex 7 M-Series HBM2E and should not be attributed to Agilex 27 ([Agilex 7 HBM2E section](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/docs/programmable/683458/current/heterogeneous-3d-stacked-hbm2e-dram.html)). Researcher inference: 921 GB/s is in the range of a single HBM3E stack or a pair of HBM3 stacks, and the Agilex 7 M-Series used HBM2e, so HBM is the obvious candidate, but the memory type, capacity, stack count and date are all "Contact Sales". A separate "Direct RF-Series" attaches data converters by UCIe chiplets, also without specifications ([WP-01358-1.0, p.9](https://docs.altera.com/api/khub/documents/hGPnldzZxr4wmKkmVpzs1w/content)).

## HyperFlow hardens the M-Series NoC into a full-chip network

Vendor specification. HyperFlow is Altera's name for a hardened network-on-chip, described as "the data movement backbone", with three components in Device Overview Table 6: a Main NoC for bulk transfers, a Register NoC for AXI-Lite control traffic, and a Fabric NoC that "extends data movement vertically through the FPGA fabric using embedded memory resources" ([Device Overview 2.1](https://docs.altera.com/r/docs/d791593/current/agilex-27-fpgas-and-socs-device-overview)). The white paper states the lineage directly: "Agilex 27 devices build on the production-proven hard memory NoC in Agilex 7 M-Series devices and extend it into a full-chip data network", with a system NoC running around the periphery that connects DDR and LPDDR controllers, PCIe, Ethernet, the HPS and the SDM, and "a distributed fabric NoC [that] runs alongside the columns of M20K and M80K memory blocks" ([WP-01358-1.0, p.7](https://docs.altera.com/api/khub/documents/hGPnldzZxr4wmKkmVpzs1w/content)). Ports are **512 bits wide**, the network inherits the M-Series NoC's "deadlock-free operation, quality-of-service (QoS) support, and low-latency cut-through", and **descriptor-driven, distributed DMA engines in hardened logic** handle memory-to-memory and endpoint-to-memory transfers across DDR, LPDDR, HPS, PCIe, embedded memory and the SDM, including remote DMA ([Device Overview 2.1](https://docs.altera.com/r/docs/d791593/current/agilex-27-fpgas-and-socs-device-overview); [WP-01358-1.0, p.7-8](https://docs.altera.com/api/khub/documents/hGPnldzZxr4wmKkmVpzs1w/content)). The white paper's Figure 10 says PCIe-to-DDR, HPS-to-DDR and "DDR straight into M20K/M80K memory" run with no soft logic in the path, and "only the memory-to-DSP datapath and the SDM connection remain in the fabric". Quartus Prime Pro and Visual Designer Studio build the NoC IP and address maps from the endpoints a designer selects, with an Interface Planner for bridge placement and a NoC Analyzer for congestion ([WP-01358-1.0, p.8](https://docs.altera.com/api/khub/documents/hGPnldzZxr4wmKkmVpzs1w/content)). The product table's "B'width Fabic (Slice) Tb/s" row runs **6.80, 11.89, 15.29 and 13.59 (12.44) Tb/s** across the four die groups rather than scaling with device size, which reads as a per-slice or per-ring figure rather than a whole-device total; Altera does not explain the term.

Vendor marketing frames this as the launch's centrepiece: "multi terabit-per-second data movement throughout the FPGA without consuming soft logic resources or introducing routing congestion", and CEO Raghib Hussain's line "Compute is one part, but data movement is the biggest problem to be solved" ([Altera newsroom](https://www.altera.com/newsroom/agilex-2x); [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/10/28/altera-unveils-agilex-2x-betting-on-ai-developers-to-expand-programmable-computing/)). Press repeated the claim; TechTimes likened it to NVLink, which is the author's analogy, not Altera's ([TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/329663/20261029/altera-launches-agilex-2x-fpgas-chip-data-network-built-physical-ai.htm)). Unpublished: NoC clock, per-port bandwidth, router count, latency, bridge counts per device, and any HyperFlow user guide.

## No node, modelled 1.8x perf/W, November tools, 2027 samples, IPO backdrop

Process node. **No Altera document, product page, FAQ, press release or press interview names a foundry or node for any Agilex 2X family.** Full-text searches of the white paper, briefs and table for "nm", "foundry", "18A", "Intel 3" and "TSMC" return nothing, and ServeTheHome, Converge Digest, TechTimes and SiliconANGLE each list the node as undisclosed ([WP-01358-1.0](https://docs.altera.com/api/khub/documents/hGPnldzZxr4wmKkmVpzs1w/content); [ServeTheHome](https://www.servethehome.com/altera-agilex-27-800gbe-1-6tbe-hyperflow/)). An earlier eeNews Europe interview with then-CEO Sandra Rivera called Intel 18A "really appealing" for next-generation products, but it predates the Silver Lake close and the current CEO and is not tied to Agilex 27 ([eeNews Europe](https://www.eenewseurope.com/en/ceo-interview-sandra-rivera-of-altera-on-the-move-to-intel-18a/)). Agilex 5 is on Intel 7 ([Agilex 5 Device Overview](https://docs.altera.com/r/docs/762191/current/agilex-5-fpgas-and-socs-device-overview)). Against Agilex 5, the block is 1.6x wider per clock and the table assumes a 17% higher clock, so one Agilex 27 block delivers about 1.87x the INT8 rate of one Agilex 5 block at datasheet conditions. The smallest Agilex 27 has 2.07x the per-clock MAC count of A5E065 and, at 63 versus 22 to 26 TOPS, roughly 2.4x to 2.9x the peak throughput. The A5E065 figures differ by implied clock only: 846 x 20 x 2 = 33,840 ops per clock gives 22 TOPS at 650 MHz and 25.99 TOPS at 768 MHz, the same 768 MHz the D-Series table uses on every row, so the two numbers are speed-grade variants and not a methodology dispute. Against Agilex 7, which has no tensor mode, the comparison is stark: A27F008 alone has more INT8 multiplies per clock (35,072) than the 34,112 INT9 operators in the largest Agilex 7, and the white paper's 8x per-block jump from "INT8 scalar 4" to "INT8 tensor 32" is the structural reason ([EEJournal](https://www.eejournal.com/article/inside-intel-agilex-fpgas/)).

Against AMD, the picture splits by format. In dense INT8 the A27F031 (222 TOPS) roughly matches the 2VE3858 (184 TOPS) and A27F180 is 6.6x it, though the Versal part is an edge device with 543,104 LUTs and the Agilex 27 top end is a 17.6 M LE infrastructure part with 24.6 Tb/s of transceivers, so the overlap region is A27F018 through A27F045. The format gap is the real finding. AMD's AIE-ML v2 tile does 512 INT8, 512 FP8, 1,024 MX6 and 512 MX9 multiplies per cycle, giving 2VE3858 369 TFLOPS in MX6 at twice its INT8 rate, and AMD's "up to 3X TOPs/Watt" headline is MX6 on Gen 2 against INT8 on Gen 1 at pre-silicon conditions of 1 GHz and 60% vector load ([AMD Hot Chips 2024, pp.7-8, 25-26](https://www.hc2024.hotchips.org/assets/program/conference/day2/21_HC2024.AMD.KnoppChu.final.pdf)). **Agilex 27 publishes no FP8, no INT4 and no mode that runs faster than INT8**, so there is no equivalent to that doubling. Note that AMD's MX6 and MX9 are the Microsoft shared-microexponent formats of arXiv 2302.08007, not OCP MX v1.0 formats, so neither vendor claims native OCP MXFP4 or MXFP8 support.

For OCP MXFP4 specifically, Agilex 27 as documented behaves like a wider Agilex 5. OCP MX fixes a block size of k=32 with an E8M0 shared scale for every concrete format ([OCP MX v1.0](https://opencompute.org/documents/ocp-microscaling-formats-mx-v1-0-spec-final-pdf)). On Agilex 5, the only published FPGA MX study found that the tensor block's shared-exponent mode can implement MXFP4 (E2M1) and MXFP6 (E2M3) at the full INT8 rate, because those elements convert to fixed-point mantissas that fit the 8-bit lanes, "but cannot be used for MXFP6 (E3M2) or any MXFP8 precision" because the converted values exceed 8 bits; the authors needed a hardware modification costing 36% DSP tile area to cover MXFP8 ([arXiv 2607.13898](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.13898)). If Agilex 27's Block FP16 tensor mode is the same construction, which is this report's inference and not Altera's statement, MXFP4 weights and activations would map onto the 32 INT8 mantissa multipliers at the full 1,211 TOPS-equivalent rate on A27F180, MXFP8 would still not fit, and an OCP k=32 block would span two 16-element column operands whose partial sums can be added before the single E8M0 scale is applied because the scale is constant across the block. That is a usable path, but it is INT8 silicon running 4-bit data at INT8 speed, the same ceiling Agilex 5 has today, with 1.6x more lanes per block.

External bandwidth frames the decode case. The white paper's 269 GB/s per device with LPDDR5 streams about 538 billion 4-bit weights per second, so a 7-billion-parameter MXFP4 model (about 3.5 GB) could be read roughly 77 times a second, a batch-1 decode ceiling near 77 tokens per second regardless of how many TOPS sit idle; the 921 GB/s M-Series option would lift that about 3.4x. HyperFlow's multi-Tb/s figure is on-chip movement and does not substitute for weight bandwidth. None of these decode numbers are Altera's, and no Agilex 2X inference software, model or benchmark exists to test them against.

## Conclusion

The honest summary is that Altera built a signal-processing DSP that happens to be a good INT8 tensor engine, and said so: the block's own documentation puts CINT16, INT18 and FIR cascades next to the tensor mode, the press release describes the tensor blocks as accelerating "signal processing applications", and the AI story at launch is HyperFlow and QuartusIQ rather than MAC density. That reading explains the format list. Every type Altera published is 8 bits or wider, the one block-floating-point mode appears to reuse the INT8 lanes, and there is no INT4 or FP8 even though Altera's own Stratix 10 NX had INT4 six years earlier and AMD's edge part has FP8 and 2x-rate MX. For a team planning MXFP4 inference on Altera silicon, the practical consequence is that the Agilex 5 mapping strategy carries straight over, with 1.6x the lanes per block and a 17% higher datasheet clock, and that the first opportunity for Altera to change the answer is the Agilex 27 DSP user guide and the Quartus early-access device files due from mid-November 2026.

Two things to watch. First, whether "BlockFP16" turns out to be per-16-element shared exponent (a near-drop-in for OCP MX with a two-column reduction) or something else, since that single definition decides whether MXFP4 runs at full rate or needs soft-logic conversion. Second, whether Agilex 25, when it gets a product table, inherits the 32-multiplier block or a reduced one; the portfolio brief's "vary by family and series" caveat leaves both open, and a mid-range part with the Agilex 27 block would land in the Versal AI Edge Gen 2 overlap region on dense INT8 while still lacking a sub-8-bit mode.
